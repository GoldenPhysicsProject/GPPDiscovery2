## 2. Exact pole-healing term

Define
\[
\boxed{
c_L(s)=
\frac{2L}{\pi^2}
\frac{s\sin^2(\pi s)}{s^2+a^2}.
}
\]

At every integer \(n\),
\[
c_L(n)=0,\qquad c_L'(n)=0.
\]
Therefore adding \(c_L\) changes neither the sampled values nor the sampled
derivatives and hence changes no CCM Hermite--Loewner matrix.

At \(s=ia\),
\[
\sin^2(\pi ia)=-\sinh^2(\pi a)=-\sinh^2(L/4).
\]
Thus the numerator of \(b_{02}+c_L\) vanishes at \(s=ia\), and similarly at
\(s=-ia\). The two nonreal poles are removable.

Consequently the pole term has an **entire Hermite representative**
\[
\boxed{
p_L(s)
=
b_{02}(s)+c_L(s)
=
\frac{L}{\pi^2}
\frac{s\{\cosh(L/2)-\cos(2\pi s)\}}
{s^2+a^2}.
}
\]

The equality uses
\[
\sinh^2(L/4)+\sin^2(\pi s)
=
\frac{\cosh(L/2)-\cos(2\pi s)}2.
\]

Hence
\[
p_L(n)=b_{02}(n),\qquad
p_L'(n)=b_{02}'(n)
\]
for every integer \(n\).

## 3. Compact sine-transform representation

The healed pole term is not merely entire. It is a compact sine transform.

For \(T>0\),
\[
\int_0^T
\cosh(a(T-x))\sin(sx)\,dx
=
\frac{s\{\cosh(aT)-\cos(sT)\}}{a^2+s^2}.
\]

Setting \(T=2\pi\) and \(a=L/(4\pi)\) gives
\[
\boxed{
p_L(s)
=
\frac{L}{\pi^2}
\int_0^{2\pi}
\cosh\!\left(
\frac{L}{4\pi}(2\pi-x)
\right)
\sin(sx)\,dx.
}
\]

Thus the apparent nonreal poles of the rational pole source are a critical
sampling artifact. On the Hermite grid, the same data are generated by an
entire odd function of exponential type \(2\pi\), with an explicit positive
compact-support sine density.

This is an exact identity; no zero information enters.

## 6. Compensated Weyl/log-derivative target

Let
\[
A_N(z)=\sum_{n=-N}^N\frac{\xi_n}{n-z},
\qquad
B_N(z)=\sum_{n=-N}^N
\frac{\widetilde b_W(n)\xi_n}{n-z},
\qquad
m_N(z)=\frac{B_N(z)}{A_N(z)}.
\]

Here \(\xi\) is the normalized null vector of
\(T_N=Q_N-\varepsilon_NI\).
The finite Pick theorem gives
\[
m_N(n)=\widetilde b_W(n),
\qquad
m_N'(n)=\widetilde b_W'(n)-\varepsilon_N.
\]

Introduce
\[
r_L(z)=\frac{L}{\pi^2}\sin^2(\pi z)
\]
and the compensated function
\[
\boxed{
C_N(z)=
m_N(z)+r_L(z)\frac{A_N'(z)}{A_N(z)}.
}
\]

Near an integer \(n\),
\[
\frac{A_N'}{A_N}
=
-\frac1{z-n}+O(1),
\qquad
r_L(z)=L(z-n)^2+O((z-n)^4).
\]
Hence
\[
C_N(n)=\widetilde b_W(n),
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
C_N'(n)
=
\widetilde b_W'(n)-\varepsilon_N-L.

Therefore \(C_N\) and the explicit entire function
\[
\boxed{

\(-N,\ldots,N\).

This explains the numerical residue-quantization phenomenon. At a pole \(t\)
of \(m_N\), i.e. a zero of \(A_N\), the pole of the logarithmic derivative has

\[

That is precisely the observed norming-constant law.

The finite numerical law is therefore equivalent to saying that the

### New proof target

Instead of proving residue quantization pole by pole, try to prove directly
that for a coordinated exhaustion
\[
\boxed{
C_N(z)-G_N(z)\longrightarrow0
}
\]
locally uniformly on one open subset of the upper half-plane.

If this holds and the denominator transforms \(A_N\) converge to the centered
completed zeta transform, then
\[
m_*(z)
=
G_*(z)-r_L(z)\frac{A_*'(z)}{A_*(z)}.
\]

Every nonreal zero of \(A_*\) would give a nonreal pole of \(m_*\), because
\(r_L(z)\neq0\) for nonreal \(z\). But a locally uniform limit of the finite
Herglotz functions \(m_N\) is Herglotz and cannot have a nonreal pole. This
would force all zeros to the real spectral axis.

This is not yet a proof: the compensated convergence is the new load-bearing
statement.
