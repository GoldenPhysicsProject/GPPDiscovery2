# Projective Fourier same-state theorem and helicity correction

## Status

This note supersedes the loose claim that the full twistor/dual-twistor Fourier reflection `k -> -k-4` by itself flips physical helicity.  It does not.

The linear split-signature Fourier/Penrose representation theorem is substantially stronger and cleaner than the previous handoff formulation, but its physical interpretation must respect the different homogeneity conventions on ordinary and dual twistor space.

## Exact external theorem used as a benchmark

Mason and Skinner, *Scattering Amplitudes and BCFW Recursion in Twistor Space* (arXiv:0903.2083), section 7 and appendix B, start from one on-shell momentum-space wavefunction

\[
\Phi_{2h}(\lambda,\tilde\lambda),
\qquad
\Phi_{2h}(r\lambda,r^{-1}\tilde\lambda)=r^{2h}\Phi_{2h}(\lambda,\tilde\lambda).
\]

They construct its dual-twistor representative by half Fourier transform in \(\tilde\lambda\),

\[
f(W)=\int d^2\tilde\lambda\,e^{i[\mu\tilde\lambda]}\Phi(\lambda,\tilde\lambda),
\]

and its ordinary-twistor representative by half Fourier transform in \(\lambda\),

\[
F(Z)=\int d^2\lambda\,e^{-i\langle\omega\lambda\rangle}\Phi(\lambda,\pi).
\]

Combining these gives the complete ambient Fourier transform

\[
f(W)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\int d^4Z\,e^{-iZ\cdot W}F(Z).
\]

If `f` has dual-twistor homogeneity `n`, then `F` has ordinary-twistor homogeneity

\[
-n-4.
\]

For a helicity-\(h\) state,

\[
n=2h-2,
\]

so the ordinary-twistor representative has degree

\[
-n-4=-2h-2.
\]

This is exactly the standard ordinary-twistor Penrose weight for the **same helicity h**.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{dual twistor }\mathcal O(2h-2)
\quad\stackrel{\mathcal F}{\longleftrightarrow}\quad
\text{ordinary twistor }\mathcal O(-2h-2)
}
\]

is a same-state/same-helicity representation change.

### Convention correction

The numerical identity

\[
-2h-2=2(-h)-2
\]

is true, but the right-hand side uses the **dual-twistor** helicity convention on a degree that is living on **ordinary twistor space**.  Reading it as a physical helicity flip mixes the two chiral conventions.

Thus the previous slogan

`Fourier k -> -k-4 therefore h -> -h`

must be replaced by:

> Full Fourier duality exchanges the ordinary- and dual-twistor homogeneity conventions while preserving the underlying on-shell momentum state.  A physical/orientation chirality flip, if present in the GPP construction, is an additional geometric reinterpretation and is not generated by Fourier homogeneity alone.

This is not a defeat of the one-geometry/two-lifts picture; it is exactly the rigorous version of it.

## The +4 is in the actual transform

The `-4` is not merely independent Serre-duality bookkeeping.  In ambient dimension four the Fourier transform of a homogeneous distribution of degree `k` has degree `-k-4`.

Mason-Skinner make this explicit.  The naive four-dimensional integral diverges for homogeneous projective data and must be interpreted distributionally.  Writing `Z=r Theta` and integrating out the radial scale produces a projective transform with the canonical top form

\[
D^3Z=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}Z^\alpha dZ^\beta\wedge dZ^\gamma\wedge dZ^\delta,
\]

of weight `+4`.

For target homogeneity `n`, the projective formula is schematically

\[
f(W)\propto \int D^3Z\,F(Z)(Z\cdot W)^n
\]

for `n <= -1`, while for `n >= 0` it contains the regularized kernel

\[
(Z\cdot W)^n(\log|Z\cdot W|-\alpha_n).
\]

The same ambient rank-four orientation form that generates the GPP `Lambda^2` pairing therefore also appears directly in the projective Fourier kernel through `D^3Z`.

## Exact Penrose-null ambiguity

For `n >= 0`, rescaling the projective representative changes the projective Fourier result by an arbitrary degree-`n` polynomial in `W`.

Mason-Skinner explicitly show that the corresponding X-ray/Penrose transform annihilates such degree-`n` polynomials.  Therefore the natural representative is defined modulo precisely a Penrose-null polynomial ambiguity.

So the quotient already formalized abstractly in

`ProjectiveFourierPenroseQuotient.lean`

is not an ad hoc physical quotient.  It matches the standard projective Fourier regularization ambiguity.

## Correct split-signature cohomological setting

Aryapoor, *The Penrose Transform in the Split Signature* (arXiv:0812.3692), gives the global scalar split transform in a cohomology-with-support formulation.  The relevant object is not naively global

\[
H^1(\mathbb{CP}^3,\mathcal O(-2)),
\]

but compact-support cohomology on the complement of the real slice,

\[
H^1_c(\mathbb{CP}^3\setminus\mathbb{RP}^3,\mathcal O(-2)),
\]

which maps isomorphically to the appropriate ultrahyperbolic solution space (with the density twists used in the theorem).  Equivalently, the split real X-ray formulation uses homogeneous data on `RP^3`.

This means the preferred rigorous GPP route is:

1. real homogeneous/distributional twistor data on `RP^3`, or the equivalent supported cohomology class;
2. projective Fourier/twistor transform with its Penrose-null representative ambiguity;
3. X-ray/Penrose reconstruction;
4. only then the GPP orientation/Hodge reinterpretation.

## What is now actually closed linearly

Mason-Skinner appendix B proves that the Fourier transform of the X-ray reconstruction is precisely the half Fourier transform to the on-shell momentum wavefunction.  For general helicity, dual-twistor functions of degree `2h-2` reconstruct the helicity-`h` massless field, and their half Fourier transform is the same `Phi_{2h}`.

Since the ordinary- and dual-twistor representatives in section 7 are obtained from that same `Phi`, the correct linear commuting statement is, modulo the conventional normalizations and representative quotient,

\[
\boxed{
P_{PT^*}(\mathcal F_{proj}F)=P_{PT}(F)
}
\]

where the two Penrose/X-ray maps use their appropriate ordinary- versus dual-twistor conventions.

This is a **same physical field** intertwiner, not yet an orientation-reversed field intertwiner.

## What remains genuinely GPP-specific

The real open bridge is now sharper:

\[
\boxed{
\text{same-state Fourier representation duality}
\quad ?\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{epsilon/Grassmannian/Hodge orientation exchange}
}
\]

or, after an independently defined orientation reinterpretation `R_o`, something of the form

\[
P_-\circ D_\epsilon = R_o\circ P_+.
\]

The Fourier transform itself supplies the representation change and the exact `-4` canonical shift.  It does **not** supply `R_o` for free.

Therefore the former missing theorem splits into two layers:

- **External/known linear layer:** projective full Fourier transform + X-ray/Penrose reconstruction + Penrose-null polynomial quotient.
- **Open GPP layer:** prove that the epsilon-generated annihilator/polarity/Hodge operation is the geometric lift whose orientation reinterpretation turns this same-state representation duality into the desired chiral/googly identification.

That is now the correct hard target.
