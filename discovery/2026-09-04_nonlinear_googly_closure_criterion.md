# Nonlinear googly closure criterion

## Result

The nonlinear googly question can be separated into a clean uniqueness theorem plus one genuinely geometric/analytic compatibility equation.

Let a nonlinear configuration `v` determine a source twistor representative `plusOf(v)` and a spacetime geometry `metricOf(v)`. Let a candidate duality transform `dualize` map the source twistor representative into the dual field class `B`.

Assume the dual Penrose square

\[
P_B(Dz)=\beta\bigl(R(P_+(z))\bigr),
\]

where `R` reverses the bulk orientation/chirality convention and `beta` maps the bulk field into the first-order spacetime datum `Gamma`.

Assume independently that the nonlinear field equation says

\[
\beta\bigl(R(P_+(plusOf(v)))\bigr)
=\Gamma[g(v)].
\]

Then

\[
P_B(D(plusOf(v)))=\Gamma[g(v)],
\]

so the duality-produced field is automatically on shell.

If `P_B` is injective on the physical/gauge quotient, then this dual representative is unique. If a right inverse exists, then it equals the explicit reconstruction

\[
B[v]=P_B^{-1}(\Gamma[g(v)]).
\]

Thus the nonlinear problem is no longer logically “construct an independent opposite chirality.” It is:

1. construct the ambient-epsilon/incidence duality `D`;
2. prove its Penrose square;
3. prove the nonlinear compatibility equation tying orientation-reversed source reconstruction to the geometry-determined first-order field.

Once those are supplied, uniqueness closes the rest.

## Conceptual consequence

This sharpens the “one geometry, two lifts” thesis. Off-shell independence of the dual variable is compatible with on-shell uniqueness. The physical issue is therefore whether the canonical duality generated by the four-dimensional orientation/incidence geometry lands on the same on-shell dual class selected by the nonlinear field equations.

The desired theorem is now more precisely

\[
P_B\circ D_\varepsilon
=
\beta\circ R\circ P_+,
\]

combined with

\[
\beta\circ R\circ P_+\circ plusOf
=
\Gamma\circ metricOf.
\]

These imply

\[
D_\varepsilon(plusOf(v))
=
B[v]
\]

on physical classes whenever `P_B` is injective.

## Hard falsifier

The proposal fails in its strongest form if any of the following occurs:

- no canonical incidence/cohomology transform `D_epsilon` exists without adding extra non-orientational structure;
- the transform exists but has the wrong homogeneous/canonical weight;
- its Penrose image is not the orientation-reversed source field;
- the nonlinear field equation selects a different dual spacetime datum;
- or the physical dual Penrose map has a nontrivial kernel after the intended gauge quotient, leaving genuine independent on-shell dual data.

The last possibility would weaken uniqueness but would still identify exactly where extra degrees of freedom enter.
