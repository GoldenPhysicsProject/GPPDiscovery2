Theta_omega(t)=xi(a+it)/xi(a-it),

Then K_omega is a self-adjoint involution:

K_omega*=K_omega,
K_omega^2=I.

Let Pi_+, Pi_- be the upper/lower Hardy projections and

Gamma=Pi_+-Pi_-.

Reflection exchanges the Hardy halves, so R Gamma R=-Gamma.

< f,(J_omega^2+I)f >

Pi_+(J_omega^2+I)Pi_+

## Clifford anticommutator form of the leakage

Let Gamma=Pi_+-Pi_- and K=K_omega as above. Since both are self-adjoint involutions,

Gamma^2=K^2=I.

For f in H2_+, Gamma f=f. Decompose Kf into its Hardy halves. Then

{Gamma,K}f
=
Gamma Kf+K Gamma f
=
Gamma Kf+Kf
=
2 Pi_+ Kf.

Because K=RU and reflection R exchanges H2_+ and H2_-,

||Pi_+Kf||
=
||Pi_-Uf||
=
||H_omega f||.

Therefore

||{Gamma,K}f||^2
=
4 ||H_omega f||^2.

Equivalently,

Pi_+ {Gamma,K}^* {Gamma,K} Pi_+
=
4 H_omega^* H_omega.

The prior identity can also be factored algebraically:

J^2+I
=
Gamma K Gamma K + I
=
Gamma K (Gamma K + K Gamma)
=
Gamma K {Gamma,K}.

Thus the RH leakage defect is exactly the failure of Gamma and K to satisfy a Clifford anticommutation relation on the causal sector.

If {Gamma,K}f=0 for every f in H2_+, then

J^2 f
=
Gamma K Gamma K f
=
-Gamma^2 K^2 f
=
-f.

So the order-four law is generated by two order-two reflections plus the single exchange sign.
