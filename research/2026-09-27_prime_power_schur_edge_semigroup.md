## 1. Dirichlet-to-Neumann composition

For an interval of length \(\ell>0\), define
\[
\Lambda(\ell)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\coth\ell & -\operatorname{csch}\ell\\
-\operatorname{csch}\ell & \coth\ell
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Take two intervals of lengths \(\ell_1,\ell_2\), glue the right endpoint of the first to the left endpoint of the second, and eliminate the common boundary value by Schur complement.

The resulting \(2\times2\) boundary matrix is exactly
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(\ell_1)\star\Lambda(\ell_2)
=
\Lambda(\ell_1+\ell_2).
}
\]

Indeed, with
\[
a_j=\coth\ell_j,\qquad b_j=\operatorname{csch}\ell_j,
\]
the internal diagonal is \(a_1+a_2\), and the effective off-diagonal entry is
\[
-\frac{b_1b_2}{a_1+a_2}
=
-\operatorname{csch}(\ell_1+\ell_2),
\]
because
\[
a_1+a_2
=
\frac{\sinh(\ell_1+\ell_2)}
{\sinh\ell_1\,\sinh\ell_2}.
\]
The diagonal entries reduce to \(\coth(\ell_1+\ell_2)\) by the same hyperbolic addition law.

Thus Schur elimination of an internal boundary is literally addition of edge lengths.

\begin{pmatrix}

Hence
\[
