# BPY one-sided tail-Gram factorization and contraction theorem

Date: 2026-09-20

This note extracts an exact semigroup structure from the v34 two-copy BPY kernel. It is a
necessary positive-half-line subproblem of the full RH reflection criterion, not by itself an
RH proof.

Let q be the BPY density entering the v34 kernel and let omega>0. For real a,b the manuscript
uses

H_omega(a,b)
=
2 int_{|a+b|/2}^infinity
sinh(2 omega y)
q(y+(a-b)/2)
q(y-(a-b)/2) dy.

## 1. Same positive orientation: exact difference of two tail Gramians

Assume a,b>=0. Then |a+b|=a+b. Set

t = y-(a+b)/2.

The two q arguments become exactly

y+(a-b)/2=t+a,
y-(a-b)/2=t+b.

Also

2 sinh(2 omega y)
=
exp(omega(a+b+2t))-exp(-omega(a+b+2t)).

Define on the real line

h_omega(x)=exp(omega x) q(x),
h_-omega(x)=exp(-omega x) q(x),

and the forward-tail map on L2(R_+,dt)

(tau_a h)(t)=h(t+a),  t>=0.

Then for a,b>=0,

boxed(
H_omega(a,b)
=
<tau_a h_omega,tau_b h_omega>
-
<tau_a h_-omega,tau_b h_-omega>
).

Thus the positive-orientation BPY kernel is exactly the difference of two observability/tail
Gramians.

## 2. Exact contraction equivalence on the cyclic tail span

Let

E_+ = span{tau_a h_omega : a>=0},
E_- = span{tau_a h_-omega : a>=0}.

Define algebraically on generators

C_tail(tau_a h_omega)=tau_a h_-omega.

Then the following are equivalent:

(A) every finite matrix [H_omega(a_i,a_j)] with a_i>=0 is PSD;

(B) the generator prescription above is well-defined and extends by continuity to a
contraction

C_tail : closure(E_+) -> closure(E_-).

Proof:
For a finite coefficient family c_i,

sum_{i,j} conj(c_i)c_j H_omega(a_i,a_j)
=
||sum_i c_i tau_{a_i}h_omega||^2
-
||sum_i c_i tau_{a_i}h_-omega||^2.

If (A) holds, the second norm is bounded by the first. In particular, a linear relation in
E_+ has zero first norm, hence zero second norm, so the prescription is well-defined. The
same inequality makes it contractive and therefore continuously extendable.

Conversely a contraction gives the displayed difference >=0 for every finite family.

This is purely Hilbert-space algebra after the exact kernel substitution.

## 3. Reflection interpretation

Because q is even in the BPY construction,

h_-omega(x)=h_omega(-x).

So C_tail sends the family of forward tails of an exponentially tilted profile to the
forward tails of its orientation-reflected profile.

This is the exact place where the question "which way is forward?" becomes an RH boundary
question rather than an analogy:

can the reflected orientation tails be obtained from the forward causal tail family by a
contractive intertwiner?

## 4. Semigroup intertwining

Let S_b denote left translation on tails,

(S_b f)(t)=f(t+b), b>=0.

On the algebraic cyclic span,

S_b tau_a h_omega=tau_{a+b}h_omega,

and hence

C_tail S_b = S_b C_tail.

Thus any positive-half-line solution is a contraction in the commutant of the unilateral
translation semigroup on the cyclic subspace generated by h_omega.

This opens a Hardy/Laplace transfer-function route: under the Laplace transform, semigroup
commutants are analytic multipliers. The problem becomes identification of the multiplier
taking the cyclic vector h_omega to h_-omega, followed by a Schur bound.

Care is required: if the cyclic vector is not outer, the multiplier is defined only on the
cyclic invariant subspace and may carry an inner/model-space defect. That is exactly the
Nyman-Burnol obstruction shape.

## 5. What this does NOT yet prove

The full BPY criterion allows arbitrary real a_i. The absolute value |a+b| in the lower
integration limit means mixed-orientation pairs are not represented by the same simple
one-sided tail feature map. The theorem above therefore proves an exact necessary
same-orientation contraction problem, not equivalence with full RH positivity.

For a,b<=0, symmetry reduces to the same positive block. Mixed-sign pairs retain the genuinely
two-sided boundary coupling and are where a full proof must still work.

Also, the pointwise relation

h_-omega(x)=exp(-2 omega x) h_omega(x)

does NOT itself prove Gram domination: multiplication by exp(-2 omega x) does not commute
with the tail translations, so cancellation between shifted cyclic vectors can be amplified.

## 6. First-order BPY Dirac system

For the v34 exponential vectors, use center/relative coordinates

c=(1-2t)(x+y)/2,
d=(x-y)/2.

The even/odd channels are

U_z=e^{i z c} cos(z d),
V_z=-i e^{i z c} sin(z d),

and obey

partial_c U + partial_d V=0,
partial_c V + partial_d U=0.

Hence every finite BPY combination obeys this same first-order 1+1 system.

If r_u(v)=q((u+v)/2)q((u-v)/2), Psi_u=sqrt(r_u), and

A_u=partial_v-Psi_u'/Psi_u,

then A_u(Psi_u f)=Psi_u partial_v f, so the weighted equations are

A_u(Psi_u U)=-partial_c(Psi_u V),
A_u(Psi_u V)=-partial_c(Psi_u U),

up to the fixed c,d versus u,v scaling convention.

The standard positive current gives

partial_c(|U|^2+|V|^2)
+
2 partial_d Re(U conjugate(V))
=0.

This controls the SUM of even and odd energies. The RH contraction requires the DIFFERENCE

||U||^2-||V||^2 >=0.

Therefore the usual positive Dirac energy current is not enough. A successful flux proof
must use the BPY weight, analyticity/exponential-subspace condition, or the global
prime-Archimedean boundary condition to control the indefinite/Krein charge.

That distinction is essential: the doubled-parent first-order algebra is now exact, but its
ordinary positive energy conservation is not secretly the RH inequality.
