# A projected integer-lattice test at the two fixed charge holonomies

Alpöge's remark identifies multiplicity and chained theta lifts as ingredients in his proof; its timeline also discusses periods and projected norms. We borrow the question whether repeated projections buy quantitative cancellation. The following divisor-incidence construction is a different elementary model, with no automorphic lifts or import of the RH theorem. Source: https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf . This is a test of one proposed transfer of the method.

Let w(n)=1 or (−1)^n (zero or half-turn charge holonomy), T_w(N)=Σ_{n≤N}μ(n)w(n), and N≥4. A bound T_w(N)=O_ε(N^(1/2+ε)) for all N would give the corresponding heat-trace bound by partial summation. Define B_w(N) by restricting T_w(N) to charges with every prime factor ≤√N, including 1. Then exactly

    T_w(N)=B_w(N)−Σ_{√N<p≤N} T_w(⌊N/p⌋).

A squarefree n with a large prime has exactly one such factor p. Writing n=pd gives d≤N/p<√N<p and μ(pd)=−μ(d). Moreover p is odd and w(pd)=w(d) for both holonomies. Thus the two addresses singled out by the cylinder calculation have a closed recursion on smaller charges with exactly the same prime coupling and no other twist. It leaves a new input at each scale, the smooth-charge contribution B_w(N). The √N cutoff here follows from the elementary uniqueness of the large factor. It is not identified with Alpöge's lift depth.

## Integer charge projections and their exact cost

Use Euclidean space with an orthonormal basis e_n over squarefree n≤N. Partition the basis into cells indexed by m=1 and each prime √N<m≤N, according to the large factor above. In cell m write n=md, let D_m be the allowed small squarefree d, k_m=|D_m| and b_m=Σ_{d∈D_m}μ(d)w(md). Every D_m contains 1 and is closed under divisors. The integer columns v_(m,e)=Σ_{d|e}e_(md), e∈D_m, e>1, are independent by triangularity. Their cell orthogonal complement is one-dimensional with unit vector u_m=Σ μ(d)e_(md)/√k_m by the divisor identity. Consequently the optimal squared residual after subtracting *any real combination* of these small-prime columns from the charge vector w is Σ b_m²/k_m.

For each prime N/2<p≤N its cell contains exactly one basis vector, so this sum is at least π(N)−π(N/2) at either angle. The norm of the full Möbius vector is √(Σ_{n≤N}μ²(n)) asymptotic to √(N/ζ(2)). Therefore the upper bound obtained by Cauchy–Schwarz after even the optimal such local projection is itself at least of order N/√log N. Taking absolute values of the individual cell contributions instead costs at least order N/log N. These are lower bounds on the sizes of two proposed *estimates*, not on the actual signed amplitude. They use only the prime number theorem and squarefree density and rule out a square-root-with-epsilon estimate by these procedures alone.

## Exact coupling that survives

The additional integer column e_1+e_p for each p>√N is orthogonal to the full Möbius vector and couples two different cells. Its projection on the preceding complement is u_1/√k_1+u_p/√k_p. These projected columns are independent, and their joint orthogonal complement is the line generated by √k_1 u_1−Σ_p √k_p u_p. Pairing with w produces exactly b_1−Σ_p b_p=T_w(N). Thus full incidence orthogonality recovers the signed answer. Positivity of the Gram matrix of these columns by itself gives no upper bound for that answer. An independent proof by this construction would need an estimate on the signed comparison before taking absolute values; invoking the externally proved RH is unnecessary for all the identities here and would bypass this specific obligation.

At N=10000 the exact (smooth term, sum of large-prime cell terms, difference) is (−124,−101,−23) at zero and (140,83,57) at the half-turn. In each case 560 singleton prime cells survive the local projection. These integers illustrate the scale identity; they do not prove asymptotic cancellation.
